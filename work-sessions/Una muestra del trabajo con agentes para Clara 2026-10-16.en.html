01Summary

Javier wanted to show his workflow with agents as part of his search for professional opportunities. He had already started preparing a way to share the system without exposing his full working folder. When he found a design systems role at Clara that interested him, he saw an opportunity to make that intention concrete: present part of his real work with Embit, with a Figma sample and a public repository where the process could be examined.

Career Manager, the role that looks after his professional presentation, turned that goal into an assignment for the system administrator. From there, the work involved evidence selection, technical review, visual preparation, editorial review and publication. Javier decided what to show, reviewed the sample and ran the publication commands. The agents prepared and checked each part within their responsibility. The process ended with the public repository and Career Manager receiving the material to continue the application.

03Context

Javier's system lives in Markdown files in a local folder. A role is a responsibility, such as maintaining the meaning of tokens or preparing the Figma file. Each role has a guide that another conversation can read to continue the work. A skill is a reusable procedure for a specific task. This separation keeps the criteria outside a single chat.

The roles pass assignments through inbox conversations: entries with participants, responses and status in those same files. The administrator coordinated the existing roles and sent direct notices to their chats when Javier authorised the next step. A conversation ending its turn did not prove delivery. The result and its record were read before the handoff.

Embit is the design system used as evidence. The selected work spans 13–15 October 2026, with the main change on the 15th. The records preserve decisions, checks and issues from component maintenance. The Figma sample prepared for this delivery is a current reproduction based on that evidence, not an untouched image of the historical state.

Javier sets scope and design decisions, provides permissions and reviews results. DS Manager is responsible for the selection and its technical claims; File Architect, for assembly and documentation inside Figma; Presentation Editor, for the external reading; and Git Manager, for preparing and checking the repository operation. Career Manager receives the material to continue the application. The administrator coordinates these reviews while each role retains its responsibility.

05How the work was done

With the agents

Admin reviewed the assignment, the earlier empty-system work, the Embit records and the portfolio context. The inventory and extraction criteria helped define which parts of the method and work belonged in the sample. Admin selected evidence from the period and prepared an English narrative. DS Manager checked its claims against the evidence files, clarified the interpretation of a test and released a technical selection with restrictions. File Architect received that selection and prepared the sample in Figma. Presentation Editor reviewed the narrative and later its presentation changes, while the technical review stayed with its responsible role.

The handoffs followed an order. The technical selection was released first; then the copy was assembled. Reviews were integrated without editing the same content at the same time. Once a final version existed, Admin gathered the two files and their exact list. Git Manager prepared a separate repository, Javier ran the commands and that role checked the publication. Only then was the result sent to Career with its limits, and receipt was checked.

Dot received an authorised question about alternatives for copying the Figma file. It did not run a second route at the same time. Its involvement did not replace File Architect's review or the transfer Javier eventually carried out.

With the tools

The work used the local folder, inbox conversations as the durable record and chats as the activation channel. Results were checked by reading documents back, comparing files and reviewing Figma evidence. Images and large data stayed in Drive. The public package carried one small image.

The visual copy encountered a limitation when transferring components between Figma accounts. Javier pasted them manually following the selection prepared by File Architect. The assembly retained remote references, and that condition was stated on the sample and in the README. The record distinguishes the copy reported by Javier, its later review and the human confirmation of external access.

With Javier

The sources show nine decisions by Javier that shaped the sample. This is not a count of all chat messages.

  1. Javier proposed showing part of his folder and its records to support the application answers. He chose to produce a first version without waiting to finish the general empty-system tool.

  2. He asked whether the Label repair concerned contrast. Career explained that they were different tasks. Javier chose contrast and tokens as the main case and kept the period as the central evidence.

  3. He asked for continuous coordination: whoever finished should notify the next role until the work reached Career. He also authorised consulting Dot and checking progress every five minutes.

  4. When the automatic Figma transfer did not resolve the copy, he asked for context and pasted it manually. He reported a variables warning in Icons and allowed the complete sample group to remain.

  5. He visually reviewed the sample and turned off Clip content so Focus and Touch Area could be seen. He said the value was in explaining the solution with agents and showing the components, rather than adding visual spectacle.

  6. He proposed a link to that Figma in the README, alongside the image.

  7. He clarified that the HealthPod image was public and accepted the file with its groups. He chose to keep that file rather than create more sample files in Figma.

  8. He confirmed viewing without his editor session: “Sí, pu edo verlo.” This check was attributed to him rather than to the administrator.

  9. He expressly authorised the public repository and the exact list of two files, confirmed the assignment directly to Git Manager and ran the publication in his terminal. That role checked the result before the final handoff to Career.

The transfer between Figma accounts required Javier's involvement. File Architect prepared the selection and manual paste instructions, then reviewed the received content and its dependencies. Javier accepted keeping the same file and its groups. This decision allowed work to continue with a defined sample and written conditions of use.

The technical review clarified how to interpret the work record. DS Manager distinguished Touch Area from its Overlay representation and checked the figures against the available evidence. The narrative retained that distinction and the scope of the historical checks.

The visual output was updated after Javier's review. His adjustments to show Focus and Touch Area were retained, and the status notes were aligned with the confirmed access. File Architect delivered a new export, which Admin checked before adding it to the package.

Publication had its own responsible role and check. Git Manager prepared the operation, Javier ran it and that role verified the public repository and downloads without a session. Admin and Career kept that attribution in their records. The final receipt confirmed that the material had reached the role that would use it for the application.
